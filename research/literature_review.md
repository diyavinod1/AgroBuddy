# Literature Review

Crop disease detection commonly uses convolutional neural networks trained on datasets such as PlantVillage. Transfer learning with MobileNet, ResNet, EfficientNet, and similar architectures is widely used because it reduces training cost while retaining strong visual features.

Conversational agricultural assistants improve usability by letting farmers ask free-form questions. Large language models can synthesize irrigation, fertilizer, and prevention guidance, but prompts must be grounded in safe, practical recommendations and should avoid unverified chemical instructions.

Speech interfaces are important in rural and multilingual settings because typing can be inconvenient and literacy levels vary. Integrating speech-to-text, text-to-speech, and local language responses improves access.

Explainability methods such as Grad-CAM help users and researchers inspect whether a visual model focuses on leaf lesions rather than irrelevant background patterns.
